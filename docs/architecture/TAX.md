# Tax on documents

Status: design, accepted for implementation on 2026-11-14. Nothing here is built until the stage that names it is merged; the stage table at the end records what is.

No sales or purchase document in Archava carries tax today. This document fixes the rules for a generic value-added tax on documents. It makes no claim about the law of any country.

## Rules

1. **Tax is the company's data.** Archava ships no rate, no code and no rule as true. A company enters its own tax codes and a named reviewer confirms each one. Every screen that shows a tax code says it was entered by the company and is not verified by Archava. Nothing is described as compliant.
2. **No code, no tax.** A company without tax codes, and a document without a tax code, behaves exactly as it does today.
3. **A tax code belongs to one book.** It has a code, a kind (`output` for sales, `input` for purchases), a rate, a base fraction, a control account and an effective date range. Ranges of one code do not overlap. A version applies only once its reviewer has confirmed it. What a stored version says never changes; a change is a new version.
4. **Rates are fractions of integers.** A rate is `numerator/denominator` (11% is `11/100`). The base fraction, `1/1` by default, scales the amount the rate applies to (`11/12` taxes eleven twelfths of the price). No floating point is used anywhere.
5. **One tax code per document, with a stated price basis.** A document is either tax-exclusive (prices are net; tax is added) or tax-inclusive (prices contain the tax; tax is extracted).
   - Exclusive: `tax = round(net × base × rate)`, `gross = net + tax`.
   - Inclusive: `net = round(gross / (1 + base × rate))`, `tax = gross − net`.
   - Rounding is half away from zero, once per document, in the document's own unit (whole Rupiah for IDR, the ISO minor unit otherwise). `net + tax = gross` always holds.
6. **A document keeps what it was taxed with.** An invoice stores the code, its version, rate, base fraction, price basis, net and tax. Later changes to the code do not change the document. A correction of the invoice is split into net and tax with the invoice's stored rate.
7. **A supplier's tax is what the supplier's document says.** The entered tax amount is stored beside the amount Archava computes from the code; the entered amount is the one posted, and a difference is shown.
8. **Tax posts to its control account through the existing source bridges.** A sales invoice posts receivable (gross) against revenue (net) and output tax. A supplier bill posts goods received not invoiced (net) and input tax against payable (gross). The tax account is the control account of the tax code version stored on the document; the posting policies keep their roles and gain none. A taxed document whose tax account cannot be posted to is refused at capture rather than posted without its tax.
9. **The tax report ties to the ledger.** For a period it shows output tax, input tax and the difference from posted sources, beside the balances of the control accounts, with any difference between the two shown.

## Not covered

- Tax codes per line, several taxes on one document, tax on tax, and exempt or zero-rated lines mixed with taxed ones.
- Withholding taxes, tax on payments, cash-basis tax, reverse charge and import tax.
- Filing, electronic invoice submission, authority-issued numbers or any connection to a tax authority.
- Tax on sales orders before invoicing, point-of-sale receipts, foodservice tickets, subscriptions and manual invoices without a company.

## Decisions taken in stage A

- **Confirmation is a second step on every book.** `tax_code.create` stores a version as `entered`; it applies only after `tax_code.confirm`. On a book with independent posting the confirmer must be another person than the one who entered it (the journal-posting rule); on any other book the same person may confirm. Creation never confirms.
- **Withdrawing.** `tax_code.retire` on an entered version withdraws it. It never applied, and it no longer holds its date range or fixes the kind of its code.
- **Retirement needs no second person.** Any holder of `tax.manage` retires a confirmed version, on every book.
- **Fractions are stored as entered.** `22/200` is not reduced to `11/100`.
- **"Applies on a date" defaults to the database server's current date.** The list returns the date it used as `appliesOn`; no tenant time zone is involved.

## Decisions taken in stage B

- **The order names, the invoice stores.** A company sales order stores the tax code text and the price basis and nothing else; its lines and `total_estimate` stay the priced integers (the net with an exclusive basis, the gross with an inclusive one). At creation the code must be a confirmed output code of the company's book that applies on the database's current date. The rate is taken when the invoice is made.
- **The invoice date is the day the invoice is created.** Billing has no document date: an invoice has `created_at`, stamped with the command's one clock sample. "Applies on the invoice date" means the database's date of that instant (`created_at::date`, the same calendar the tax code list uses). If no confirmed version applies on it the invoice is refused; it is never created untaxed. The version row is share-locked while the invoice is written.
- **Columns.** `gross_minor` stays the amount the customer owes and stays immutable. `tax_minor` is the tax part of it and is immutable with the snapshot (code id, code, version, kind, rate and base fraction, price basis); the net is `gross_minor − tax_minor` and is not stored. `correction_tax_minor` is the tax part of `correction_minor`, and each correction row stores its own `tax_minor`. The tax of the document after its corrections is `tax_minor + correction_tax_minor`. This mirrors the existing pair `gross_minor` / `correction_minor` instead of making `tax_minor` a running amount.
- **A correction is a gross amount, split by the corrected total.** `append_correction` changes what the customer owes on either price basis. Its tax part is `contained(gross + corrections after) − (tax + correction tax before)`, where `contained(g) = g − round(g / (1 + base × rate))`. On the gross of an exclusive document `contained` returns exactly the tax that was added, so one rule serves both bases, the parts of all corrections add up to the tax of the corrected total, and a total of zero has net and tax of zero. A single small correction can therefore carry no tax, or all of one unit; the sum is what is exact. The database recomputes the invoice's correction tax on every update and checks each correction row against it.
- **The database is the authority.** Billing 009 recomputes the tax from the order total and the stored fractions with integer arithmetic, requires the snapshot to be the order's tax code and basis and a confirmed output version of the company's book that applies on the invoice date, and compares the order total with `gross − tax` (exclusive) or `gross` (inclusive). A manual invoice cannot carry tax.
- **Not postable yet (removed in stage C).** A taxed invoice was refused when the company's book has an invoice posting policy, at creation and again at issue (a policy can be set between the two), so the invoice bridge never captured a taxed invoice as if it were untaxed. Billing 010 removes this guard.
- **The Sales screen reads its tax codes from the Commerce workspace.** For the selected company the read returns `taxCodes`: code, version, rate, base and range of the confirmed output versions that apply today. It needs the Commerce `orders.read` grant and access to the company; it does not need the Accounting `tax.read` grant, and it returns no account and no person.
- **The checkout document refuses a taxed order.** It shows a draft or reserved order's total, and such an order has no tax amount yet (rule "Not covered": tax on sales orders before invoicing). `POST /embed/checkout/sign` answers 409 `checkout_order_taxed` instead of signing the order total.
- **Both members or neither.** `taxCode` without `priceIncludesTax`, or the reverse, is a conflict with its own reason, not a validation error. Only `order.create_for_company` has the two members.

## Decisions taken in stage C

- **The tax account is the tax code's.** Output tax posts to the control account of the tax code version stored on the invoice. The invoice posting policy keeps its three roles (receivable, revenue, payment clearing); there is no tax role.
- **What is captured.** A source stores `tax_minor`, the tax part of its amount in ledger minor units with the policy's unit factor (×100 under `whole_idr`, ×1 under `minor`), and the tax control account with the revision that account has at capture. The tax part is what Billing stored: the invoice's `tax_minor` for an issue, the correction row's `tax_minor` for a correction, minus `tax_minor + correction_tax_minor` for a void, and nothing for a collection or refund. The database recomputes it on every insert, so a source whose tax differs from its invoice cannot be stored, also by direct SQL as the serving role.
- **The journal.** Issue: debit receivable (gross), credit revenue (net), credit the tax control account (tax). A correction or a void uses the same three accounts with its own gross, net and tax; a negative amount swaps the sides. A journal line cannot be zero, so a source whose tax is zero has two lines, and a correction that is all tax has no revenue line. Collections and refunds are unchanged.
- **Three reviewed accounts.** `invoice_source.draft` takes exactly the accounts of the journal: two for a source without tax, three (with the tax control account at its current revision) for a source with tax. Anything else is refused as `invoice_source_tax_accounts_required` for a source with tax and as a plain conflict for one without. The command itself now accepts two or three accounts; three for an untaxed source used to be an invalid submission and is now that conflict.
- **A 0% code.** The invoice has a tax code and tax 0. Its issue source keeps the code's control account (so a later correction has it), has `tax_minor` 0 and drafts with two accounts. The account checks below apply to it as to any taxed invoice.
- **Refusals at capture.** Issuing a taxed invoice on a book with an invoice policy is refused, and the billing command rolls back with nothing written, when the tax code version is not a confirmed or retired output code of the book (`invoice_tax_code_not_postable`), while its control account is inactive (`invoice_tax_control_account_changed`), and when that account is one of the policy's accounts (`invoice_tax_control_account_in_policy`). Only the second can be reached through the commands: an output code needs a liability account, the policy an asset and a revenue account, and an account keeps its class, so the third is a database rule that no command can trip; the first needs a tax code row changed outside its guards.
- **A control account deactivated after confirmation.** The stored code is unchanged and still applies. Capture compares only that the account is active: an account's identity and class never change, so its revision is not compared with the one the code was confirmed with. Issuing an invoice taxed with that version is refused while the account is inactive and nothing is written; reactivating the account releases the invoice, including a draft created earlier, and the source stores the account's current revision. A new tax code version with another account serves later invoices only, because a draft keeps the version it was created with.
- **Corrections keep the issue's tax account.** A correction or void takes the tax account of its issue source, as it takes the policy snapshot, and is not checked against the account's current state. If the account is inactive by then the draft is refused by the journal line guard, as it is for a policy account today.
- **Dates.** A source is dated by the UTC date of its billing receipt, as before. The tax code version was chosen on the invoice's creation date in the database's calendar (stage B) and is not evaluated again at capture. The two calendars can differ around midnight, and an invoice issued days after it was created posts under the version of its creation date: the invoice's stored snapshot governs.
- **Earlier taxed invoices stay outside the ledger.** A taxed invoice issued before the book had an invoice policy is not captured later, like any invoice issued before the policy; its corrections, payments, refunds and void are not captured either. Its tax reaches the ledger only through opening or manual work.
- **Reports.** The receivable subledger, its control comparison, aging, the close guard and close readiness count captured amounts, which are gross: unchanged. Revenue in the trial balance and profit and loss comes from posted lines and is net for a taxed source. Nothing was changed there.

## Decisions taken in stage D

- **What is entered.** `document.capture` and `document.revise` take `taxCode` (the code text of an input tax code of the company's book) and `taxMinor` (the tax amount printed on the supplier's document, in the document's unit: whole Rupiah for IDR, the ISO minor unit otherwise). Both or neither; one without the other is a conflict with its own reason, not a validation error. `declaredTotal` and the claimed line prices stay the net of goods. The amount owed to the supplier is `declaredTotal + taxMinor`. There is no price basis on a purchase.
- **The document date chooses the version.** The version stored is the confirmed input version of the company's book that applies on the document's own `documentDate`, not on the day the claim is entered. If none applies the command is refused (`supplier_document_tax_code_unknown`, `_not_input`, `_not_confirmed`, `_not_applicable`); the claim is never stored untaxed. The version row is share-locked while the claim is written.
- **Columns.** `supplier_documents` gains `tax_minor` (entered), `tax_computed_minor` (`round(declared_total × base × rate)`, the exclusive rule) and the snapshot (code id, code, version, rate and base fraction). A version snapshot is the whole row, so every version keeps the tax it was captured with. The read returns `tax` with `enteredMinor`, `computedMinor`, `differenceMinor` (entered less computed) and `totalMinor`; a document without tax has no `tax` member and is read as before.
- **Entered is not compared with computed.** The database recomputes the computed amount and checks the snapshot against the tax code version (Purchasing 008), as Billing 009 does for invoices, but any entered amount within the bound is accepted and is the amount posted.
- **The bound.** The entered tax is not negative and not above the document's goods total. No rate or base fraction exceeds one, so no code can give more than the net; an amount above it is refused as `supplier_document_tax_amount_out_of_bounds`. The bound also keeps "a source with tax has goods": a bill never consists of tax alone.
- **Tax is fixed per version.** A match, a hold and a void keep the tax of the version they act on. Changing the tax is a correction: `document.revise` states the code and amount of the new version, or none. A matched claim is voided first, as for any correction. Nothing is carried over: a revision without the two members is a claim without tax.
- **Matching is unchanged.** The exact-goods policy compares quantities, unit prices and the net total. Tax is not part of the evidence and never holds a claim.
- **The tax account is the tax code's.** Input tax posts to the control account of the tax code version stored on the matched document version. The purchase posting policy keeps its four roles; there is no tax role.
- **What is captured.** A bill source stores `tax_minor`, the entered tax in ledger minor units with the unit factor of the policy the order's origin pinned (×100 under `whole_idr`, ×1 under `minor`), and the tax control account with the revision that account has at capture. `goods_minor` stays the net. A void stores the negated tax of the matched version it reverses and the tax account of that bill's source. A receipt carries no tax. `purchase_effect` is not changed; the tax part comes from `purchase_effect_tax`, which reads the document version Purchasing stored. The database recomputes it on every insert, so a source whose tax or tax account differs from its document cannot be stored, also by direct SQL as the serving role.
- **The journal.** Bill: debit goods received not invoiced (net goods), debit the tax control account (tax), credit payable (goods plus tax). A void reverses all three. A journal line cannot be zero, so a source whose tax is zero has the two lines it had before.
- **Three reviewed accounts.** `purchase_source.draft` takes exactly the accounts of the journal. For a bill or void with tax anything else is refused as `purchase_source_tax_accounts_required`; without tax the refusal is the plain conflict it was. The command already accepted two or three accounts.
- **A 0% code.** The document has a tax code and tax 0. Its bill source keeps the code's control account, has `tax_minor` 0 and drafts with two accounts. The account checks below apply to it as to any taxed bill.
- **Refusals at capture.** Matching a taxed claim of an order that posts purchases is refused, and the purchasing command rolls back with nothing written, when the tax code version is not a confirmed or retired input code of the book (`supplier_bill_tax_code_not_postable`), while its control account is inactive (`supplier_bill_tax_control_account_changed`; reactivating the account releases the claim and the source stores the account's current revision), and when that account is one of the policy's accounts (`supplier_bill_tax_control_account_in_policy`). Unlike on the sales side the third can be reached through the commands: an input code needs an operating asset account and the policy's inventory account is one.
- **Payables are gross.** What is owed for a bill is its goods plus its tax: `payable_balance` (the payment limit, the payables summary and the void guard), the payable positions and their aging, and the payable control comparison count `goods_minor + tax_minor`. A payment up to the gross is accepted and one above it refused. Nothing derived a bill amount from the supplier document's goods total. Close readiness, the close guard and source coverage count sources, not amounts, and a source with tax always has goods: unchanged.
- **The form reads its codes from the Purchasing workspace.** For the selected company the read returns `taxCodes`: code, version, rate, base and date range of every confirmed or retired input version of the company's book, because the version is chosen by the document date. It needs the Purchasing `documents.read` grant and access to the company; it does not need the Accounting `tax.read` grant, and it returns no account and no person.
- **Earlier taxed documents stay outside the ledger.** A claim of an order confirmed before the book had a purchase policy is not captured, taxed or not, as before.

## Open questions

- **Which calendar dates a document.** Orders and invoices use the database server's date, like the tax code list. The accounting bridge dates a source by the UTC date of its receipt. Stage C keeps both and lets the invoice's snapshot govern (see its decisions); a tax report by period (stage E) has to say which date it groups by.
- **A taxed draft whose code is retired before issue.** The snapshot is taken at creation; issuing later does not look at the code again.
- **Tax on the checkout document.** Showing an estimate with today's version would put an amount in a signed document that the invoice may not carry. Refusing is the stage B answer; an estimate would need the net, tax and total in the signed string.
- **A correction entered as a net amount.** `append_correction` takes what the customer owes less or more. A user who thinks in net amounts on an exclusive document has no way to enter one.
- **The tax report and uncaptured invoices.** Taxed invoices issued before the policy are outside the ledger (stage C decision). Stage E has to say whether the report lists them as a difference.
- **A 0% invoice and the account check.** An invoice taxed at 0% is refused at issue while its code's control account is inactive, although nothing would post to that account. Kept for consistency in stage C.
- **A correction after the tax account was deactivated.** It is captured with the issue's account, its draft is refused by the journal line guard with the general reason, and the unposted source blocks the period close until the account is reactivated. Policy accounts behave the same today.
- **The agent's draft schema.** `invoice_source.draft` now takes two or three accounts; nothing tells the agent which a source needs. Proposals are still reviewed by a person.
- **A supplier document date in another period.** The tax code version is chosen by the document date; the bill source is dated by the UTC date of the match. A document dated in an earlier period posts its tax in the period of the match. Stage E has to say which date the tax report groups input tax by.
- **Entered tax far from computed.** Any entered amount up to the goods total is accepted and posted. No tolerance holds a claim or asks for a second review when the difference is large; the difference is only shown.
- **Tax on other supplier charges.** Freight, discounts and other charges are still outside the supplier document, so a supplier's tax on them cannot be entered except as part of the one tax amount, where it is bounded by the goods total.
- **A 0% supplier document and the account check.** As on the sales side, a claim taxed at 0% is refused at matching while its code's control account is inactive, although nothing would post to that account.
- **A void after the tax account was deactivated.** It is captured with the bill's account; its draft is refused by the journal line guard with the general reason, and the unposted source blocks the period close until the account is reactivated. Policy accounts behave the same today.
- **A supplier document matched twice.** A bill that was posted, voided, corrected and matched again has two bill sources for one document. The payable subledger report keys its positions by document and refuses to answer in that case (a conflict); this is so with or without tax and was not changed in stage D. See [supplier payments](../modules/SUPPLIER_PAYMENTS.md#tax-on-a-supplier-bill).
- **The agent's draft and claim schema.** `purchase_source.draft` takes two or three accounts and a claim two optional tax members; nothing tells the agent which a source needs or which codes exist. Proposals are still reviewed by a person.
